## Findings

- Two DGX Spark (GB10, 128 GB each, TP=2 over 200GbE) prefill Qwen3-32B in vLLM (NVFP4); a **Mac Studio M4 Max 128 GB** decodes with MLX (4-bit). Each TP rank pushes its KV shard to the Mac over **ZMQ on 10GbE**; the Mac preallocates and injects into an MLX KVCache.
- Source-reported crossover (200 generated tokens, 3-run mean): P/D wins total time at ctx 256–8192; DGX-only wins at 16384 and 32000; Mac-only OOMs at 32K. Example at ctx 8192: Mac TTFT 44.30 s, P/D TTFT 4.22 s (prefill 3.70 s, transfer 0.52 s), DGX TTFT 0.87 s. At ctx 256 the KV transfer is 0.022 s.
- A reported root cause for an earlier slowdown: a **KV cache dtype mismatch on injection triggered an MLX slow path**; forcing bf16 injection restored native decode speed.
- The README offers a static "router recommendation" by context length and output length, with a band (8192 < ctx < 16384) marked "measure".
- Limitations stated: 10GbE ceiling (~1.2 GB/s), FP16 transport cast to BF16, different quantizations on the two sides, chunked prefill disabled due to a vLLM connector bug, single request serial.

## Limitations of this reading

README only; the numbers are the authors'. The hardware pairs an NVIDIA box with a 128 GB Mac Studio; nothing here is reproducible on one M4 laptop.

## What this source blocks

- "Disaggregated prefill/decode with KV transfer to a Mac" exists with a published crossover table. The question "when is KV transfer cheaper than recompute" has a measured answer for this one setup; a MacReduce version would be a replication on different hardware unless it contributes a decision procedure that generalizes across prompt length, link rate, and output length.
- The dtype-mismatch finding is a concrete example of a hidden runtime state that a scheduler must observe rather than assume.
