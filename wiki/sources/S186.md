## Findings and limitations

**Source-reported:** FFA-LoRA keeps the randomly initialized A frozen and trains and aggregates only the zero-initialized B. Aggregation is then exact, because averaging B with a shared A equals averaging B A, and the trainable and communicated parameters halve. The paper argues FFA-LoRA behaves like LoRA with an infinitely large scaling factor, which removes alpha tuning. In the non-private QNLI rows with RoBERTa, FFA-LoRA at rank 8 scored 91.62 against LoRA's 91.36, and rank 2 dropped to 88.56 against 91.76.

**Limitations:** experiments are classification fine-tuning of RoBERTa and LLaMA-family models, mostly under differential privacy, not language-model perplexity on WikiText-2 with a 4-bit base. Freezing A changes the model class, so a MacReduce comparison needs its own single-node FFA control rather than the existing full-LoRA curve. All clients must share the same A seed. This is the "freeze one factor" baseline already named in [adapter aggregation](../guides/optimizers-adapters.md); it is not a MacReduce contribution. No external benchmark performance has been reproduced by this reading.
